### Selection rationale

We selected the MSU-IIT venue booking system because it is highly relevant to the team's immediate environment, giving us direct access to key stakeholders such as student organizers, faculty, and administrative staff, for testing and feedback. Building a scheduling and booking application is highly feasible for a student team to prototype within a single semester. In contrast, the sari-sari store app carries a high risk of poor user adoption due to technological barriers (e.g., lack of smartphones or internet), and the waste management app relies too heavily on external local government units (LGUs) to maintain accurate data, posing a significant external dependency risk for a time-boxed academic project.

## 3. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

CATS (Campus Activity & Time-slot Scheduler)

### Problem statement

When a student organization hosts an upcoming event, part of the preparation process is booking the venue where said event will take place. This process mainly works by word of mouth from the school offices and departments that the student organizers will go to, and sometimes, such words may be inaccurate that leads to a source of confusion and a waste of time for both the student organizers and school offices. This inconvenience can lead to students having to push back their academic requirements to comply with the now-corrected instructions or have the event completely cancelled. By creating an application that aims to streamline the booking process, this problem can be avoided.

### Proposed solution

By creating a digitalized application where student organizers can view what venues are available, when it is available, and what documents need to be submitted to which offices to successfully book a reservation, the issue of confusion leading to a waste of time will gradually lessen over time as the application gets incorporated. By having an accessible instructions roadmap, no more will the student organizers have to rely on word of mouth from different offices that sometimes conflict with one another, which leads to  unnecessary paperworks. Less unnecessary paperworks mean less work for the organizers and less work for the officers that will have to review it.

## 5. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting
The intended users of CATS are student event organizers from recognized student organizations, societies, and executive councils at Mindanao State University–Iligan Institute of Technology (MSU-IIT). The system will also support authorized administrative personnel, such as staff from the Office of Student Development Services (OSDS), department representatives, and personnel responsible for campus facilities. It will serve as a centralized platform where users can check venue availability, review reservation procedures, identify required documents, and monitor the progress of venue booking requests. For this semester's version, CATS will focus on simplifying the initial venue reservation process within the campus through a functional prototype using sample data and verified information where available. 

### In-scope capabilities
1. Venue availability viewing. Allow student organizers to view a list of campus venues, their basic details, and their availability on selected dates and time slots to help them identify suitable locations for their events.

2. Reservation request submission. Allow student organizers to submit a venue reservation request by providing essential details, such as the event name, purpose, expected number of attendees, preferred venue, date, and time.

3. Reservation status tracking. Allow organizers to view the status of their submitted requests, such as pending, approved, or rejected, with remarks provided by the reviewing personnel when applicable.

4. Booking procedure and document guidance. Provide a centralized guide outlining the required documents, approval sequence, and responsible offices involved in the reservation process, helping organizers avoid confusion caused by inconsistent instructions.

5. Basic administrative management. Allow authorized administrators to review reservation requests, update their statuses, and maintain venue availability records within the prototype.
### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. Full integration with official university systems. The initial version will not integrate with existing university databases, student information systems, or official administrative platforms because access and authorization may be difficult to obtain within the semester.

2. Online payment processing. The system will not handle venue rental fees, deposits, reimbursements, or other financial transactions, even if certain venues require payment.

3. Automated approval through all university offices. The system will not replace official signatures, physical document submissions, or existing approval procedures. It will provide guidance and basic status tracking rather than automatically securing every required authorization.

4. Mobile application deployment and push notifications. The project will focus on a functional web-based prototype instead of developing separate Android and iOS applications or implementing a complete push-notification service.

5. Live synchronization with official venue schedules. The prototype will not automatically retrieve real-time availability from university calendars or facility management systems. Availability information will depend on the records maintained within the prototype and must not be treated as an official confirmed booking unless authorized personnel verify it.


### Expected deliverables

The team expects to produce the following deliverables by the end of the project:
Functional CATS prototype: A web-based application demonstrating venue browsing, reservation request submission, status tracking, booking procedure guidance, and basic administrative management.

Project documentation: A document explaining the problem, objectives, target users, system requirements, scope, limitations, and proposed system workflow.

Interface and workflow designs: Preliminary interface mockups and a diagram illustrating how organizers submit requests and how administrative personnel review them.

Sample venue and reservation records: A sample dataset for demonstrating venue availability, reservation requests, and status updates without relying on unauthorized access to actual university records.

Testing and evaluation report: Documentation of test cases, observed results, identified defects, and feedback gathered from potential users if consultations are conducted.

Source code and repository: The project's source files maintained in a shared or designated GitHub repository, together with instructions for running the prototype.

## 6. Review and Next Steps

### Change made after reviewing the proposal

- Before review: CATS was initially envisioned as a digital venue booking application that would allow student organizers to check availability and obtain the instructions and documents needed to reserve campus venues. The initial concept could be interpreted as a fully automated booking system connected to official university schedules and approval processes.

- After review: The project will focus on a functional web-based prototype that provides venue availability information, reservation request submission, status tracking, booking procedure guidance, and basic administrative management. Official university system integration, online payments, and automated approvals across all offices will be excluded from the initial version.

- Reason: This revision makes the project more realistic for a student team working within a limited development period. It preserves the primary goal of reducing confusion and unnecessary delays while recognizing that official booking procedures, venue schedules, and approval authority must be verified with the appropriate university offices. It also gives the team specific features that can be developed, demonstrated, and evaluated without requiring immediate access to internal university systems.


### Biggest remaining risk

The biggest remaining risk is the team's lack of verified information about the official venue reservation process at MSU-IIT, including actual venue availability, required documents, approval sequences, and the responsibilities of each office. If the team relies on incomplete or inaccurate information, CATS could reproduce the same confusion it is intended to address. Furthermore, if the university already uses an established reservation process or system, the proposed application may need to complement rather than duplicate existing procedures.
To address this risk, the team should consult student organization officers and relevant administrative personnel as early as possible. The team should verify which information can be published, determine whether a digital request-tracking prototype would be useful, and clearly distinguish confirmed procedures from sample or unverified information.

### Recommended next steps
Conduct initial consultations with student organizers and the relevant university offices to validate the problem and understand the current booking workflow.

Confirm the minimum set of venue details, reservation fields, document requirements, and request statuses needed for the prototype.

Finalize the system requirements and interface mockups based on the information collected.

Divide development tasks among team members and implement the core reservation workflow using sample data.

Test the prototype against the success indicators, document the findings, and revise the system based on observed usability issues.
